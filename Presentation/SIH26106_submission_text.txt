=== IDEA TITLE ===
Security-Copilot: Agentic AI for Email Threat Detection, Geolocation & Forensic Intelligence

=== ABSTRACT / SUMMARY ===
Security-Copilot is an AI-powered email threat detection and forensic intelligence platform that answers the three questions every cyber-crime investigator actually needs answered: Is this email malicious? Where did it really come from? And who is behind it — and what else is it connected to?

Email is the front door of cyber fraud. Indians lost ₹22,495 crore to cyber fraud in 2025 (MHA / I4C), and Business Email Compromise alone cost victims $3.04 billion worldwide (FBI IC3 2025). Yet today's filters only block or deliver a message. They leave no forensic trail, analysts still check headers, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, IP addresses and WHOIS by hand across five or more separate tools, and AI-written lures, lookalike domains and relay chains slip past static rules. Related frauds are never linked into campaigns.

Security-Copilot closes this gap with a two-speed architecture:

1. Instant protection where people read mail. A Gmail Add-on automatically scans every new email and labels it Dangerous, Suspicious or Safe inside the inbox, while a Chrome extension checks every website before the click and automatically checks each Gmail message the moment it is opened. These instant verdicts come from a combined AI decision engine: Jev, our primary phishing classifier, decides the text verdict, informed by an ONNX URL model corroborated by VirusTotal, with a local BERT model as an always-available backup. The result: zero false alerts in our live Gmail testing.

2. Deep forensic investigation on demand. An autonomous LangGraph AI agent investigates an email the way a trained analyst would — tracing the Received hop chain back to the originating server, verifying SPF, DKIM and DMARC with domain alignment, geolocating the origin IP (city, ISP, ASN) and flagging TOR, VPN and hosting infrastructure, visiting every link in a sandboxed headless browser, checking domain age and reputation, and scanning attachments in a sandbox (real file type, macros, ClamAV signatures, and PDF text, links and embedded JavaScript) without ever opening or executing them.

Every case ends in an explainable verdict in the five classes the problem statement asks for — legitimate, suspicious, impersonated, phishing, fraud-related — with a 0–100 risk score in which every point carries a written reason, an attribution of the likely actor (spoofed domain, compromised account, anonymised infrastructure or direct actor), an origin map, a campaign ID that links it to related frauds using SecureBERT embeddings and DBSCAN clustering, and a one-click, court-ready forensic PDF report.

Privacy and legality are built in: PII is masked with Microsoft Presidio while headers are preserved as evidence, cases auto-delete after 90 days, and confirmed threats are reported and blocked — never attacked back. The design is DPDP Act 2023-aligned and supports CERT-In's 6-hour incident-reporting mandate.

This is not a concept — it is built and running. All 6 problem-statement components are delivered, powered by 12 AI and forensic tools, 26 automated tests, and 4 entry points (Gmail Add-on, Chrome extension, Next.js analyst dashboard and Python CLI). It needs ₹0 of paid threat-intelligence APIs and runs on an ordinary CPU, open-source and self-hostable. Next, we will anchor every case's evidence on a blockchain-backed, hash-chained ledger for tamper-proof chain of custody, extend ingestion to Outlook and IMAP, and pilot with colleges, cyber cells and banks on the way to national scale.

Security-Copilot turns every suspicious email from a dead end into evidence: Detect → Trace → Attribute → Report.

────────────────────────────────
2. OUR SOLUTION
────────────────────────────────
Security-Copilot is an agentic AI platform that investigates an email the way an expert cyber-forensics analyst would, and does it automatically.

Drop in a raw email (.eml), a URL or pasted text — or simply receive mail in Gmail. An AI agent traces its route, verifies the sender, geolocates the origin, checks every link and attachment, remembers past cases, and returns a classified, attributed, explainable verdict with a complete forensic report.

It works at two speeds:
• INSTANT (seconds, automatic): every incoming email and every website visit gets an instant verdict, right inside Gmail and the browser.
• DEEP (on demand): a full forensic investigation by an autonomous AI agent, streamed live to the analyst dashboard, ending in a court-ready report.

────────────────────────────────
3. HOW IT WORKS — ARCHITECTURE
────────────────────────────────
A. FOUR ENTRY POINTS
• Gmail Add-on (Google Apps Script): a background auto-scanner checks new mail every minute and applies bold, coloured Dangerous / Suspicious / Safe labels directly in the inbox. A sidebar card on every open email shows its verdict instantly and, with one click ("Check Report"), launches a full investigation.
• Chrome MV3 extension: checks every page before the user interacts with it, shows a quiet toast when a page is safe and a persistent warning banner when it is not, automatically checks each Gmail message the moment it is opened (once per message, never repeated), supports Gmail, Outlook web, Yahoo Mail and Proton Mail from the popup, and offers one-click Report & Block.
• Analyst dashboard (Next.js): .eml upload, live step-by-step investigation progress, full forensic case pages, campaign views and one-click PDF reports.
• Python CLI: URL and raw-email investigations straight from the terminal, for automation and SOC workflows.

B. FAST TRIAGE — THE COMBINED AI DECISION ENGINE (instant)
• Link check: every URL — the page being visited or each link inside an email — is scored by a local ONNX phishing-URL model and corroborated by VirusTotal. VirusTotal can both escalate a threat the model missed and overrule a false alarm on a legitimate domain.
• Text verdict: Jev (typesafe/jev-1.13, served through OpenRouter's Decisions API) is our primary phishing classifier. It judges the intent of the email — is it trying to trick the reader into giving credentials, money or a click? — not its grammar, which makes it robust against flawless AI-written lures. Jev also receives the link verdict as context, so its reading of the text is informed by what the links really are. A local BERT phishing model runs alongside as a backup that takes over whenever Jev is unreachable.
• Combined verdict: the text and link signals are fused into one Safe / Suspicious / Dangerous decision, shown as a banner in the browser and a coloured label in Gmail.
• Accuracy: pairing Jev with VirusTotal-corroborated link scoring removed the false positives that single models produce on ordinary bank notifications and legitimate login pages. The result is zero false alerts in our live Gmail testing — users only see a warning when it matters.

C. FULL INVESTIGATION — THE AUTONOMOUS AI AGENT (on demand)
Built with FastAPI and LangGraph, with every step streamed live to the dashboard over Server-Sent Events.
• Router: known-bad blocklist and a 24-hour verdict cache give repeat threats an instant answer.
• Deterministic forensics — always run on a raw email, never left to the AI's discretion:
  – Header & protocol analysis: From, Return-Path, Reply-To, Message-ID and the full Received chain, read oldest-first to find hop-0, the true originating server; out-of-order hops and sender mismatches flagged.
  – Sender authentication: SPF evaluated against the delivering IP, DKIM signature verification and DMARC with domain alignment. The receiving server's own verdict is kept beside our live re-check, so rotated DKIM keys or changed DNS never erase evidence.
  – Origin traceability: country, region, city, ISP and ASN of the origin IP via MaxMind GeoLite2 (offline), correlated with the Tor Project exit list, VPN and datacenter IP ranges and AbuseIPDB; sender-domain age, MX and TXT records add infrastructure context.
  – Attachment & PDF sandbox: SHA-256/MD5 hashes, real file type versus claimed extension (catches invoice.pdf.exe), Office macro detection, ClamAV signature scanning, and a sandboxed read of PDFs that extracts their text and links, runs them through the same phishing and link-reputation checks, and detects embedded JavaScript, auto-launch actions and hidden embedded files — all without ever opening, rendering or executing the file.
• LLM investigation agent: reasons over all the evidence and decides which tool to use next, looping until it is confident. Its toolkit: a sandboxed headless Chromium browser (screenshots, forms, hidden assets, hosting IP), WHOIS + VirusTotal domain reputation, ONNX + BERT classifiers, brand-impersonation web search, IP geolocation, entity correlation across past cases, and SecureBERT recall of similar past investigations.
• Verdict rules: a transparent rule engine converts evidence into a 0–100 risk score where every point has a written reason (for example, a DMARC failure adds 30 points and an SPF hard-fail adds 20), escalates the verdict when hard evidence contradicts the AI, and assigns attribution.
• Fail-safe by design: if the AI provider is down or rate-limited, the system fails safe to "suspicious" — never "legitimate" — and every external lookup degrades independently, so one outage never breaks an investigation.

D. EVIDENCE, PRIVACY & CASE MEMORY
• SQLite case history with raw headers preserved as evidence.
• PII in email bodies masked with Microsoft Presidio before storage.
• SecureBERT case embeddings give the platform a memory of every past investigation.
• A NetworkX entity graph links senders, domains and IPs seen together; DBSCAN clustering groups related malicious cases into campaigns with a shared campaign ID.
• 90-day retention with automatic deletion.

E. ANALYST-FACING RESULT
• Explainable verdict in the five PS classes, with risk score, written reasons and attribution.
• Case page: origin on an interactive map, hop-by-hop trace path, SPF/DKIM/DMARC chips, attachment findings and a connections graph to past cases.
• Campaigns table: clusters of related frauds with shared domains, IPs and senders, first and last seen.
• One-click forensic PDF report with every field an investigator needs.
• Gmail labels and sidebar card for end users; browser warning with Report & Block.

6. DEMO CASE — A SPOOFED PAYPAL EMAIL SENT THROUGH TOR
────────────────────────────────
An email titled "Urgent: your account has been limited" claims to come from PayPal, carries a lookalike link and an attachment named invoice.pdf.
• Header analysis finds hop-0 at 185.220.101.34 and replies routed to a lookalike domain instead of paypal.com.
• Authentication: DMARC fails for paypal.com (policy p=reject) and SPF hard-fails — the sender is not authorised to send for PayPal.
• Origin: Brandenburg, Germany — flagged as a TOR exit node, so the origin is shown as anonymised.
• Attachment: "invoice.pdf" is actually an executable (invoice.pdf.exe), detected from its real file type without opening it.
• Verdict: phishing, risk score 100/100, attribution: spoofed domain, automatically linked to campaign C-9b6bad01 with related cases — delivered with a one-click forensic PDF.

How we handle the hard problems:
• TOR / VPN hides the origin → flagged as anonymised infrastructure; the origin is shown as "probable", never overstated.
• Flawless AI-written lures → Jev judges intent, not grammar; headers, authentication and links are judged independently of the text.
• LLM error or outage → rule-based scoring continues and the system fails safe to "suspicious".
• Weaponised attachments → sandboxed read of text, links and embedded scripts; files are never opened or run.
• Sensitive personal data → PII masked, 90-day auto-delete.
• API cost and rate limits → offline GeoIP, caching, and optional (not required) paid APIs.
• DKIM key rotation / DNS changes → the receiving server's original verdict is preserved beside the live re-check.
